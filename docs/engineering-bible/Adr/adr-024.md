# ADR-024 — Adopt a Layered Rate Limiting and Abuse Protection Strategy

## Status

Accepted

---

# Context

Public-facing marketplaces are frequent targets for:

- Credential stuffing
- Brute-force attacks
- API abuse
- Web scraping
- Denial-of-service attempts
- Spam
- Resource exhaustion

Because Ruvo intentionally allows anonymous browsing, additional protections are required to preserve availability without degrading the experience for legitimate users.

---

# Decision

Ruvo will implement layered rate limiting and abuse protection across the platform.

Rate limiting is enforced at multiple levels depending on the type of resource and user context.

Limits are adaptive rather than globally uniform.

---

# Protection Layers

Rate limiting may consider:

- IP address
- Authenticated user
- API key (future)
- Endpoint sensitivity
- Request frequency
- Geographic anomalies
- Device characteristics (where appropriate)

No single identifier is relied upon exclusively.

---

# Endpoint Categories

## Public Read APIs

High limits with caching support.

Examples:

- Listing search
- Property details

---

## Authentication APIs

Strict limits.

Examples:

- Login
- Password reset
- Verification code requests

---

## User Actions

Moderate limits.

Examples:

- Messaging
- Inspection requests
- Reports

---

## Administrative APIs

Restricted access with enhanced monitoring.

---

# Abuse Response

Depending on severity, the platform may:

- Delay responses
- Reject requests
- Require CAPTCHA (future)
- Temporarily block clients
- Generate security alerts

Responses are proportional to observed behaviour.

---

# Rationale

## Availability

Protects platform resources for legitimate users.

---

## Security

Reduces automated attacks and credential abuse.

---

## Fair Resource Usage

Prevents a small number of clients from consuming disproportionate resources.

---

## Future Scalability

Supports adaptive policies as traffic grows.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## No Rate Limiting

### Pros

- Simplest implementation.

### Cons

- High abuse risk.
- Poor availability.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Global Static Limits

### Pros

- Easy to configure.

### Cons

- Too restrictive for some endpoints.
- Too permissive for others.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Better platform stability.
- Reduced abuse.
- Improved availability.
- Predictable infrastructure usage.

## Negative

- Legitimate users may occasionally encounter limits.
- Requires monitoring and tuning.

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Sensitive endpoints use stricter limits.
- Limits are observable through monitoring.
- Administrative overrides are audited.
- Rate limiting must fail safely without exposing sensitive information.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Provides a reliable experience even during periods of malicious or excessive traffic.

## Engineering Impact

Requires centralized rate limiting infrastructure and endpoint classification.

## Operational Impact

Introduces monitoring, alerting, and policy management responsibilities.

# AI Implementation Guidance

Never apply identical limits to every endpoint.

Classify endpoints according to risk and usage patterns.

Ensure rate limiting complements—not replaces—authentication and authorization.

## Definition of Done

Every externally accessible endpoint has an appropriate abuse protection policy, and platform behaviour remains stable under excessive or malicious traffic.
