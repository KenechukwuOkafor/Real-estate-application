# ADR-007 — Adopt Lifecycle-Based Data Management with Soft Deletes as the Default Deletion Strategy

## Status

**Accepted**

---

# Context

Ruvo is a trust-based real estate marketplace.

The platform manages information that has long-term business value, including:

- Listings
- Agent accounts
- Verification records
- Property media
- Reports
- Conversations
- Payments
- Inspections
- Subscriptions

Simply deleting database records creates several risks:

- Loss of evidence during disputes.
- Broken relationships between records.
- Inaccurate analytics.
- Difficulty recovering from accidental deletion.
- Reduced auditability.

The platform therefore requires a data lifecycle strategy that preserves historical information while allowing users to remove content from active use.

---

# Decision

Ruvo will adopt **Lifecycle-Based Data Management**.

By default, records will be **soft deleted** rather than permanently removed.

Soft deletion means:

- The record remains in the database.
- The record is hidden from normal application queries.
- Relationships remain intact.
- Audit history is preserved.
- Authorized administrators may restore the record when appropriate.

Permanent deletion is reserved for exceptional cases and follows a controlled process.

---

# Data Lifecycle

Every major entity progresses through a lifecycle rather than existing only as "active" or "deleted".

Example:

Draft

↓

Pending Review

↓

Approved

↓

Published

↓

Archived

↓

Soft Deleted

↓

Permanently Deleted (exceptional)

Deletion is therefore treated as a state transition rather than destruction.

---

# Entities Covered

Soft deletion applies to:

- Listings
- Property Images
- Property Videos
- Agents
- User Profiles
- Conversations
- Reports
- Inspection Requests
- Notifications
- Subscription Records (where appropriate)

Each domain may define additional lifecycle states, but permanent deletion remains exceptional.

---

# Permanent Deletion Policy

Permanent deletion is permitted only when required by:

- Legal obligations.
- Privacy regulations.
- User data export and erasure requests.
- Administrative maintenance.
- Data retention policies.

Permanent deletion must be deliberate, auditable, and irreversible.

---

# Rationale

## 1. Marketplace Trust

Historical records are valuable when investigating:

- Fraud
- Duplicate listings
- Agent misconduct
- Customer complaints
- Payment disputes

Removing evidence weakens trust in the platform.

---

## 2. Auditability

Audit trails require stable references.

Soft deletion preserves historical relationships without affecting active operations.

---

## 3. Recovery

Users occasionally delete listings by mistake.

Soft deletion enables straightforward restoration without reconstructing lost data.

---

## 4. Analytics

Historical trends depend on complete datasets.

Deleting records permanently introduces gaps that distort reporting.

---

## 5. Operational Simplicity

Recovering a soft-deleted record is far simpler than restoring data from backups.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Hard Deletes

### Pros

- Simpler schema.
- Reduced storage usage.

### Cons

- Permanent data loss.
- Broken audit history.
- Irrecoverable user mistakes.
- Poor moderation support.

**Decision:** Rejected.

---

## Archive Tables

### Pros

- Smaller active tables.
- Clear separation between active and historical data.

### Cons

- More complex synchronization.
- Duplicate schemas.
- Increased migration complexity.

**Decision:** Deferred.

---

## Event Sourcing

### Pros

- Complete historical record.
- Rich audit capabilities.

### Cons

- Significant architectural complexity.
- Excessive for Ruvo's current scale.

**Decision:** Rejected for MVP.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Preserves business history.
- Enables restoration.
- Supports moderation.
- Improves analytics.
- Maintains referential integrity.

---

## Negative

- Tables grow larger over time.
- Queries must exclude soft-deleted records by default.
- Data retention policies become more important.

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- User-facing queries exclude soft-deleted records by default.
- Administrative interfaces may include deleted records.
- Foreign key relationships remain intact.
- Permanent deletion requires explicit authorization.
- Every deletion action must be audited.

---

# Implementation Strategy

Every soft-deletable entity includes:

```sql
deleted_at TIMESTAMPTZ NULL
deleted_by UUID NULL
deletion_reason TEXT NULL
```

A record is considered active when:

```sql
deleted_at IS NULL
```

No boolean `is_deleted` flag will be used, as timestamps provide both state and deletion time.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Users gain protection against accidental deletion while preserving marketplace integrity.

---

## Engineering Impact

Developers must account for lifecycle states in queries and APIs, but benefit from easier recovery and debugging.

---

## Operational Impact

Moderators and support staff can investigate historical records without restoring database backups.

---

## Risk Assessment

### Risks

- Accidentally exposing soft-deleted records.
- Growing database size.
- Inconsistent lifecycle handling across domains.

### Mitigations

- Repository-wide query conventions.
- Shared query helpers.
- Automated tests for lifecycle filters.
- Periodic archival reviews.

---

# Future Considerations

Future enhancements may include:

- Automated archival after inactivity.
- Configurable retention policies.

- GDPR-compliant deletion workflows.
- Legal hold mechanisms.

# AI Implementation Guidance

## Context

Soft deletion is the default deletion strategy for Ruvo.

Deletion should almost always be implemented as a lifecycle transition rather than a destructive operation.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

- Never hard delete user-generated content without explicit architectural approval.
- Filter soft-deleted records from public queries.
- Preserve referential integrity.
- Record who performed the deletion and why.
- Audit every deletion event.

## Common Mistakes

- Using `DELETE FROM` for user-facing entities.
- Forgetting to filter `deleted_at IS NULL`.
- Breaking foreign key relationships.
- Treating deletion as irreversible.

## Definition of Done

Entities transition cleanly through their lifecycle, soft-deleted records remain recoverable, and permanent deletion is reserved for exceptional, audited scenarios.
