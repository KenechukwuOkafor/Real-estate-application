# ADR-022 — Adopt Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)

## Status

Accepted

---

# Context

Different categories of users require different capabilities.

Examples include:

- Visitors
- Customers
- Agents
- Moderators
- Administrators
- Super Administrators

Managing permissions individually for every user becomes difficult to maintain as the platform grows.

A scalable authorization model is required.

---

# Decision

Ruvo adopts **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)**.

Permissions are assigned to roles.

Users receive permissions by belonging to one or more roles.

Authorization decisions evaluate permissions rather than user identity alone.

---

- Anonymous browsing

---

- Favourites
- Inspection requests

---

Agent

- Listing management
- Media uploads

---

- Content moderation

---

Administrator

- User management
- Verification

---

Super Administrator

- Platform-wide operational control
- Emergency access
- Infrastructure management

---

# Permission Principles

Permissions are:

- Explicit
- Least privilege
- Auditable
- Version controlled

Users never receive implicit administrative access.

---

# Rationale

## Scalability

Adding new users requires assigning roles rather than redefining permissions.

---

## Security

Least privilege reduces accidental privilege escalation.

---

## Maintainability

Centralized role definitions simplify permission management.

---

## Auditability

Administrative actions can be traced to role assignments.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Permission Per User

### Pros

- Highly flexible.

### Cons

- Difficult maintenance.
- Increased complexity.
- Inconsistent authorization.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Hardcoded Permissions

### Pros

- Simple implementation.

### Cons

- Poor scalability.
- Difficult to modify.
- Increased technical debt.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Predictable authorization.
- Cleaner security model.
- Easier onboarding.
- Better operational control.

## Negative

- Role design requires careful planning.
- Future custom roles may require additional architecture.

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Every protected action maps to explicit permissions.
- Administrative roles require MFA.
- Role assignments are auditable.
- Privileges follow the principle of least privilege.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Users receive capabilities appropriate to their responsibilities.

## Engineering Impact

Authorization logic becomes consistent across services.

## Operational Impact

Simplifies administration and security reviews.

# AI Implementation Guidance

Never authorize actions based solely on user type strings.

Always evaluate permissions through the RBAC model.

Avoid hardcoding privileged behavior inside application logic.

## Definition of Done

All protected operations are authorized through explicit roles and permissions, with complete auditability of role assignments.
