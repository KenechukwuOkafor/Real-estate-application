# ADR-018 — Adopt a Moderated Listing Approval Workflow Before Publication

## Status

Accepted

---

# Context

Publishing property listings directly after submission increases the risk of:

- Scam listings
- Duplicate properties
- Misleading information
- Offensive content
- Policy violations
- Low-quality marketplace data

A curated marketplace provides greater long-term value than an unrestricted one.

---

# Decision

Every newly submitted listing enters a moderation workflow before becoming publicly visible.

Publication is an explicit administrative approval, not an automatic consequence of submission.

---

# Listing Lifecycle

```
Draft

↓

Submitted

↓

Under Review

↓

Approved
        │
        ├────────► Published
        │
        ▼
Rejected

↓

Archived

↓

Deleted
```

Each state transition generates audit events and notifications.

---

# Review Criteria

Moderators evaluate:

- Listing completeness
- Image quality
- Video quality
- Property accuracy
- Pricing plausibility
- Policy compliance
- Duplicate detection
- Required metadata

Moderators review presentation quality, not legal ownership.

Ownership verification remains a separate workflow.

---

# Moderation Outcomes

A moderator may:

- Approve
- Reject
- Request changes
- Archive
- Escalate

Reasons are recorded and communicated to the listing owner where appropriate.

---

# Rationale

## Marketplace Quality

Manual review improves the consistency and professionalism of public listings.

---

## Fraud Prevention

Scam listings are intercepted before reaching users.

---

## User Experience

Users spend less time filtering unreliable content.

---

## Brand Protection

High-quality listings reinforce Ruvo's reputation as a trusted marketplace.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Automatic Publication

### Pros

- Instant publishing.
- Minimal operational effort.

### Cons

- High fraud risk.
- Reduced trust.
- Poor content quality.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Community Moderation

### Pros

- Distributed review.

### Cons

- Slow.
- Inconsistent.
- Reactive rather than preventative.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Higher listing quality.
- Better trust.
- Reduced fraud.
- Improved user satisfaction.

## Negative

- Longer publication time.
- Moderator workload.
- Additional operational cost.

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Public visibility requires approval.
- Every moderation action is auditable.
- Rejections include documented reasons.
- Moderation status is independent of agent verification.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Creates a curated marketplace with consistently higher-quality listings.

## Engineering Impact

Requires moderation queues, lifecycle management, and notification workflows.

## Operational Impact

Establishes a structured content review process.

# AI Implementation Guidance

Treat listing moderation as a workflow with explicit state transitions.

Avoid coupling listing approval to agent verification.

Every moderation decision must produce audit events and user notifications.

## Definition of Done

Listings progress through the documented moderation lifecycle, are reviewed before publication, and all decisions remain traceable.
