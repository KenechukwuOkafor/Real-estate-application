# ADR-028 — Adopt Event-Driven Internal Communication Between Domains

## Status

Accepted

---

# Context

As Ruvo grows, business domains become increasingly interconnected.

Examples include:

- Listing approval triggering notifications.
- Subscription activation updating agent capabilities.
- Inspection confirmation sending reminders.
- Media publication updating listing visibility.
- Payment success activating subscriptions.

Direct coupling between domains creates rigid dependencies and makes the system difficult to evolve.

---

# Decision

Ruvo adopts an event-driven architecture for internal domain communication.

Domains communicate by publishing business events rather than directly invoking one another whenever asynchronous processing is appropriate.

Each domain owns its own data and reacts to events independently.

---

# Event Flow

```
Business Action

↓

Domain Event Published

↓

Event Bus / Queue

↓

Interested Consumers

↓

Independent Processing
```

Multiple consumers may respond to the same event without modifying the originating domain.

# Design Principles

## Loose Coupling

Domains should know as little as possible about one another.

---

## Single Responsibility

Each domain publishes its own business events.

Consumers decide how to respond.

---

## Event Immutability

Published events are historical facts.

They are never modified.

---

## Event Versioning

Breaking changes require new event versions.

Existing consumers remain compatible.

---

# Rationale

## Scalability

New consumers can be added without modifying existing producers.

---

## Extensibility

Future capabilities such as analytics, AI, notifications, and integrations subscribe to existing events.

---

## Fault Isolation

Consumer failures do not interrupt the originating business process.

---

## Domain Independence

Teams can evolve individual domains without tight coordination.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Direct Service Calls

### Pros

- Straightforward implementation.

### Cons

- Tight coupling.
- Cascading failures.
- Difficult scaling.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Shared Database Integration

### Pros

- Simple data access.

### Cons

- Breaks domain boundaries.
- Increases coupling.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Better scalability.
- Independent evolution.
- Easier integrations.
- Improved resilience.

## Negative

- Additional operational complexity.
- Event ordering considerations.
- Eventual consistency between domains.

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Events represent completed business facts.
- Consumers remain independent.
- Events are versioned.
- Failed consumers never invalidate completed business operations.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Supports richer features without increasing architectural complexity.

## Engineering Impact

Encourages modular domain design and independent evolution.

## Operational Impact

Requires event monitoring, replay strategies, and consumer observability.

# AI Implementation Guidance

Publish business events after successful state transitions.

Avoid direct cross-domain dependencies when event-driven communication is appropriate.

Treat events as immutable historical records.

## Definition of Done

Business domains communicate through versioned, observable events that preserve loose coupling and support independent evolution.
