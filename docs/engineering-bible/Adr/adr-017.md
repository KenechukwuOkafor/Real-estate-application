# ADR-017 — Adopt Manual Agent Verification as the Foundation of Marketplace Trust

## Status

Accepted

> **Referenced by ADR-034.**
>
> ADR-034 makes verification approval the moment an agent receives listing
> quota. Approval therefore governs two things: whether an agent may submit at
> all, and how many listings they may submit. Verification remains the
> independent gate — quota never substitutes for it.

---

# Context

Real estate marketplaces are highly susceptible to:

- Fake agents
- Duplicate identities
- Scam listings
- Impersonation
- Misrepresentation

Allowing anyone to immediately publish listings without identity verification undermines trust and damages the platform's reputation.

For Ruvo's target markets, trust is a competitive advantage rather than an optional feature.

---

# Decision

Agents must complete a manual verification process before they receive verified status and access to publishing privileges beyond the initial onboarding workflow.

Verification is performed by authorized Ruvo administrators using submitted documentation and supporting evidence.

Verification is a prerequisite for displaying the **Verified Agent** badge.

---

# Verification Objectives

The verification process confirms:

- Identity
- Contact information
- Business legitimacy (where applicable)
- Required documentation
- Platform policy compliance

Verification evaluates the agent—not individual properties.

---

# Verification Workflow

```
Registration

↓

Document Submission

↓

Administrative Review

↓

Additional Information (if required)

↓

Approved
        │
        ├──────────────► Verified Agent
        │
        ▼
Rejected
```

Verification decisions are recorded and auditable.

---

# Verification Statuses

Every agent exists in one of the following states:

- Unverified
- Pending Review
- Additional Information Required
- Verified
- Suspended
- Revoked

State transitions require explicit administrative action.

---

# Rationale

## Marketplace Trust

Verified identities increase confidence for buyers, renters, and landlords.

---

## Fraud Reduction

Manual review reduces fraudulent accounts before they become active participants.

---

## Operational Control

Verification provides an opportunity to enforce marketplace standards before publication.

---

## Reputation

Trustworthy marketplaces attract higher-quality listings and long-term users.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Automatic Verification

### Pros

- Faster onboarding.

### Cons

- Greater fraud risk.
- Easier abuse.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Community Verification

### Pros

- Scalable.

### Cons

- Slow reputation building.
- Vulnerable to manipulation.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Higher trust.
- Lower fraud.
- Better listing quality.
- Stronger brand reputation.

## Negative

- Slower onboarding.
- Administrative workload.
- Operational costs.

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Verification decisions are auditable.
- Verification badges cannot be self-assigned.
- Revocation immediately removes verified status.
- Verification records are retained for audit purposes.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Strengthens marketplace credibility and encourages user confidence.

## Engineering Impact

Requires verification workflows, state management, and administrative tooling.

## Operational Impact

Introduces a structured verification review process.

# AI Implementation Guidance

Treat verification as a controlled workflow rather than a simple boolean field.

Verification state changes must generate audit events and notifications.

Verification decisions must remain traceable.

## Definition of Done

Every verified agent has successfully completed the documented review process, and all verification actions are fully auditable.
