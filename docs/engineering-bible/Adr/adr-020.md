# ADR-020 — Adopt a Subscription-Based Marketplace Revenue Model

## Status

Accepted

> **Qualified by ADR-034.**
>
> This ADR describes subscriptions as the entitlement to publish. Until billing
> exists, entitlement is also satisfied by free listing quota granted at
> verification approval. ADR-034 is explicit that quota must not become a
> subscription tier: when billing lands, quota remains the founding-agent and
> administrative-grant path, not a free plan.

---

# Context

Real estate marketplaces typically monetize through:

- Commission per transaction
- Pay-per-listing
- Advertising
- Subscription plans

Commission models require visibility into transactions that frequently occur outside the platform.

Pay-per-listing discourages inventory growth.

Advertising can negatively affect user experience.

Ruvo requires a predictable and scalable revenue model aligned with agent success.

---

# Decision

Ruvo will monetize through recurring subscription plans for agents.

Subscriptions grant publishing privileges and marketplace features rather than charging commission on completed transactions.

---

# Subscription Principles

Subscriptions determine:

- Listing limits
- Active listing capacity
- Premium features
- Business tools
- Analytics access
- Priority support

Subscription status never determines property visibility ranking.

Search ranking remains independent.

---

# Subscription Lifecycle

```
Trial

↓

Active

↓

Grace Period

↓

Expired

↓

Reactivated
```

Subscriptions are managed independently of listings.

---

# Business Rules

- Listings remain visible after expiration unless otherwise specified by policy.
- Expired subscriptions prevent creation of new listings.
- Existing data is preserved.
- Subscription history is retained for audit purposes.

---

# Rationale

## Predictable Revenue

Recurring subscriptions create stable income.

---

## Incentive Alignment

Ruvo succeeds when agents continue using the platform rather than only when transactions occur.

---

## Marketplace Growth

Subscriptions encourage agents to build long-term inventory.

---

## Simplicity

Billing is easier to understand than commission calculations.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Commission-Based Model

### Pros

- Revenue scales with successful transactions.

### Cons

- Difficult verification.
- High operational overhead.
- Frequent off-platform transactions.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Pay-Per-Listing

### Pros

- Simple pricing.

### Cons

- Discourages listing growth.
- Inconsistent revenue.

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Advertising

### Pros

- Additional income.

### Cons

- Poor user experience.
- Misaligned incentives.

Decision:

Deferred.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Predictable recurring revenue.
- Easier financial forecasting.
- Simpler billing.
- Better alignment with agent success.

## Negative

- Requires subscription management infrastructure.
- Ongoing payment processing.

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Subscription status is independent of search ranking.
- Billing history is retained.
- Expiration never deletes user data.
- Payment failures are auditable.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Encourages long-term platform engagement while maintaining a fair marketplace.

## Engineering Impact

Requires subscription lifecycle management, recurring billing, and entitlement checks.

## Operational Impact

Introduces customer billing support and subscription administration.

# AI Implementation Guidance

Treat subscriptions as entitlement systems rather than payment records.

Avoid coupling billing logic directly to listings.

Always preserve historical subscription data.

## Definition of Done

Subscriptions govern platform capabilities through a managed lifecycle while remaining independent of marketplace ranking and listing visibility.
