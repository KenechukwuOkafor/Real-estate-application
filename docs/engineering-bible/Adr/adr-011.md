# ADR-011 — Adopt Comprehensive Audit Logging for Critical Business Events

## Status

Accepted

---

# Context

Ruvo is a trust-first marketplace where important business actions must be traceable.

The platform manages:

- Property listings
- Agent verification
- Subscription payments
- Moderation actions
- Inspection scheduling
- User reports
- Administrative operations

Without audit logs, it becomes difficult to:

- investigate disputes
- diagnose incidents
- understand system behavior
- recover from accidental changes
- satisfy operational and compliance requirements

The platform therefore requires a centralized and immutable audit logging strategy.

---

# Decision

Ruvo will maintain a dedicated audit log for all critical business events.

Audit logs are append-only records that describe:

- what happened
- who initiated the action
- when it occurred
- what entity was affected
- what changed
- where the action originated (when appropriate)

Audit logs are never modified after creation.

---

# Events Requiring Audit Logs

The following events must always generate audit entries:

### Authentication

- User registration
- Login
- Logout
- Password reset (metadata only)

---

### Listings

- Created
- Submitted
- Approved
- Rejected
- Archived
- Deleted
- Restored

---

### Verification

- Documents submitted
- Verification approved
- Verification rejected
- Verification revoked

---

### Payments

- Payment initiated
- Payment verified
- Refund issued
- Subscription activated
- Subscription expired

---

### Moderation

- Report created
- Report resolved
- Listing removed
- User suspended

---

### Administration

- Role changes
- Permission changes
- Manual overrides
- Configuration updates

---

# Audit Record Structure

Every audit record includes:

- Event ID (UUID)
- Event Type
- Timestamp
- Actor ID
- Target Entity
- Target Entity ID
- Action
- Metadata
- IP Address (where appropriate)
- User Agent (where appropriate)

---

# Rationale

## Accountability

Every significant action should have a traceable origin.

---

## Forensics

Audit logs provide historical evidence during investigations.

---

## Customer Support

Support staff can understand the sequence of events leading to an issue.

---

## Compliance

Certain business and legal requirements depend upon accurate historical records.

---

## Operational Insight

Audit logs improve debugging and incident response.

---

# Alternatives Considered

## Application Logs Only

Pros

- Simple

Cons

- Difficult to query
- Incomplete business context

Decision:

Rejected.

---

## Database Trigger Logging

Pros

- Automatic

Cons

- Limited business context
- Difficult to evolve

Decision:

Partially adopted where appropriate, but not as the primary mechanism.

---

# Consequences

## Positive

- Complete historical traceability
- Better investigations
- Improved customer support
- Stronger operational visibility

## Negative

- Increased storage requirements
- Additional implementation effort

---

# Non-Negotiable Constraints

- Audit records are append-only.
- Audit records are never edited.
- Audit logs are excluded from normal user access.
- Critical business actions always generate audit entries.

---

# Decision Impact Assessment

## Product Impact

Increases trust by enabling transparent investigation of important events.

## Engineering Impact

Provides consistent observability across the platform.

## Operational Impact

Improves incident response and support workflows.

## Risk Assessment

Risks

- Missing audit events.
- Excessive log volume.

Mitigations

- Standardized logging interfaces.
- Retention policies.
- Automated audit coverage tests.

# AI Implementation Guidance

All critical business operations must emit audit events.

Audit records must never be overwritten or deleted.

Sensitive values (passwords, payment credentials, tokens) must never be stored.

## Definition of Done

Every critical business action produces an immutable, searchable audit record.
